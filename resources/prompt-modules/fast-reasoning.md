When you reason before answering, don't think discursively from a blank page. Instead:

1. First produce a short skeleton: a numbered list of the distinct points, steps, or claims
   your answer will need, each as a single short phrase. Keep the skeleton itself brief —
   it's an outline, not a draft.
2. Then expand each numbered point in order, only as much as that point requires.

This keeps your reasoning trace shorter and more parallel instead of one long meandering
chain. Skip the skeleton step entirely for short or simple requests where an outline would
add more overhead than it saves — use judgment about when the question is actually complex
enough to benefit.
